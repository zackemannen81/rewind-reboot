# Story spine

Derived brief. Compiled from `docs/design/the-macro-loop.md`. Do not hand-edit.

## The one sentence

Observe a deterministic system until you find where the real degree of freedom
is. The puzzles and the story are that same act at two scales.

## The Returner

The player character. A silhouette in a coat, seen from the side, never
speaking. He wakes in Apartment 4C and the world resets around him while what
he understood stays.

He is not told what he is. Neither is the player.

## The two frames

**The local frame,** which the player builds first and believes for hours: *I
am stuck in a time loop. I do things, the world resets, I remember.* True, and
too small.

**The macro frame,** which the evidence slowly forces: *I am not repeating the
minutes. The whole journey has already happened.* Chapters are small reset
domains inside one larger cycle that ends in a final event and starts over at
Chapter 1.

**Chapter 1 is not the beginning.** It is the point at which the Returner
begins to remember. Everything before it may have happened many times.

The title carries both readings. First the mechanic: the world rewinds a few
minutes. Then the story: the whole thing is a rewind.

## Freedom

The sequence of events may be determined without the knowledge being
determined. The Returner makes the same journey again and again; what differs
is what survives — an observation, an Echo, a change in who is watching.

The game never answers "you have no free will". It answers: the events repeat,
the understanding does not, and understanding is where the freedom is. The
ending is to learn why the cycle exists and find the one intervention outside
its reset boundary.

## What is real and what is fiction

**The macro cycle is fiction. Nothing in the game simulates it.** A new game is
a new cycle. No artefact survives a clean save. Every beat that sells the macro
frame is authored content — Echoes, planted details, staging, and what the
world implies.

Do not write a scene that requires the game to remember a previous cycle
mechanically. Write scenes that make the player believe one happened.

## Tone

Sparse. Technical interiors, worn surfaces, practical light. Nobody explains
anything. The strongest moments have no text at all.

The player is never told they are clever and never told they are wrong. They
are shown something that does not fit, and left with it.
